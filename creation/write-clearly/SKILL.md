# Write clearly

Help the intended reader understand the technical meaning without guessing. Apply this to a draft or while writing; preserve the requested document type, voice, language and scope.

## Establish the reader's footing

Identify what the reader needs to do or understand, what they plausibly already know, and which missing concepts are necessary for this passage. A job title alone does not establish knowledge of this particular system. State consequential assumptions, introduce prerequisites before relying on them, and link to background when a full explanation would distract. Do not explain every familiar term or silently invent a reader's expertise.

## Make relationships visible

- Introduce a necessary unfamiliar term in context before using it; use one name per concept and distinguish similar terms. Expand an acronym when useful to this audience. Preserve exact identifiers and established product terminology.
- Make the actor, action and affected object clear where they matter. Prefer concrete verbs to abstract noun chains. Use passive wording when the actor is unknown, irrelevant or the object deserves emphasis; do not guess the actor.
- Resolve pronouns and vague references when more than one antecedent is plausible. Put a condition before the instruction it governs. Make cause, sequence, contrast and scope explicit where the reader would otherwise have to infer them.
- Untangle sentences that carry multiple independent ideas. Split or use a list when that makes the relationship easier to follow, but retain qualifications with the claim they limit. Shorter is not better if it loses a necessary connection.
- Give each paragraph a discernible purpose and arrange its sentences so the reader can follow the point, evidence or example. Move unrelated material or split a dense paragraph at a change of thought; do not enforce a sentence count or a mandatory topic sentence.
- Use examples to expose a specific distinction or operation. Label invented or simplified examples, and avoid presenting them as evidence of product behavior.
- Choose prose, a list, a table or a diagram according to the relationship the reader needs to understand. Keep comparable list items grammatically parallel, introduce lists and tables with their purpose, and do not turn connected reasoning into fragments just to shorten it.
- For diagrams and other visuals, state what the reader should learn from them and explain important information in nearby text. Do not rely on color alone to distinguish meaning.

## Edit without changing the facts

Read the passage from the reader's starting point, then check terms, sentences and paragraphs. For Chinese, reconstruct the information order and explicit relationships in natural Chinese instead of copying English syntax; retain technical identifiers and useful search terms. Apply the user's or project's style guide where compatible with the task.

Preserve conditions, negation, quantities, uncertainty, technical constraints and the strength of claims. Never turn an inference into a guarantee or fill a factual gap for fluency. If the source is ambiguous, keep the ambiguity visible and flag a focused question or alternative interpretations instead of choosing one silently. For an edit, return the requested revised text; briefly identify material unresolved ambiguities or substantive changes when useful. A readability pass does not verify technical correctness: check authoritative evidence when available and distinguish unverified claims from verified ones. When AI helps draft or edit, verify every fact, condition, identifier and degree of certainty against the source; fluent output is not evidence.
